## 1. Market Environment & Fundamentals

The macroeconomic landscape for the US dollar remains characterized by selective economic resilience amid mixed data points. While US construction spending experienced a significant contraction of 1.9% in September against an estimated 0.3% expansion, the manufacturing sector continues to demonstrate robust health. The October ISM manufacturing PMI beat consensus estimates, printing at 55.5 versus the expected 54.5, signaling sustained expansion in the industrial sector. Additionally, the S&P Global final manufacturing PMI for October settled at 54.9, maintaining an expansionary posture despite a minor downward revision from the preliminary 56.0. This divergence highlights a bifurcated but resilient economy where manufacturing acts as a vital anchor, supporting the USD against the Japanese Yen.

Adding to the favorable risk environment, easing diplomatic tensions in the Middle East and a subsequent decline in oil prices have spurred a rebound in US equities. This geopolitical de-escalation reduces the risk premium embedded in energy markets and supports a broader risk-on sentiment, which is highly supportive of the USD/JPY pair. From a fundamental perspective, the monetary policy divergence between the Federal Reserve and the Bank of Japan remains the primary structural driver. Hawkish commentary from Fed officials, combined with resilient US data, reinforces the outlook for higher-for-longer US interest rates, keeping the yield differential highly attractive and underpinning the bullish thesis for USD/JPY.

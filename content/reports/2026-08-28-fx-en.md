## 1. Market Environment & Fundamentals

The macro backdrop continues to favor a structurally elevated USD/JPY corridor, with rate differentials remaining a primary driver. The U.S. Treasury's $44 billion 7-year note auction clearing at a high yield of 4.512% reinforces the message that term premia in the United States are stabilizing at restrictive levels, limiting the BoJ's capacity to engineer a sustained JPY appreciation without explicit yield curve control adjustments. Federal Reserve commentary from Collins and Hammack, while acknowledging that absent new tariff and oil shocks disinflation can resume, has notably hedged the timeline, with Hammack suggesting 2.5% as a realistic 2027 floor. This measured tone preserves a hawkish bias in front-end USD pricing, constraining the JPY's recovery potential.

Cross-currency dynamics further underpin the pair's resilience, as the USDCAD's pullback into a support cluster reflects broad-based dollar profit-taking rather than a structural USD reversal. Against this, the JPY remains burdened by structurally negative real yields, an underwhelming domestic demand profile, and the absence of hawkish BoJ guidance. Until Japanese authorities signal an explicit exit from accommodation or intervene with verifiable effectiveness, the path of least resistance for USD/JPY remains skewed to the upside, with intervention risk only materializing on a discrete acceleration through psychological resistance zones.

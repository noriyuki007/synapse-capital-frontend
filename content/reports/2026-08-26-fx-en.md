## 1. Market Environment & Fundamentals

The macro backdrop remains a rates-differential story. Richmond Fed's Collins reiterated that inflation remains too high, reinforcing the policy divergence between the FOMC and the BoJ. This stance has continued to support a structurally higher US-Japan front-end yield gap, keeping USD/JPY firm. On the data side, the Richmond Fed manufacturing composite edged down to +4 from +5, but the print remains expansionary and does not materially alter the policy path. US equities are rotating off the lows with Nvidia rebounding after a seven-day slide, which reduces immediate risk-off demand for the yen and allows the pair to retain a positive beta to global risk appetite.

Geopolitics introduces a secondary catalyst. Iran and Oman have proposed a joint temporary shipping lane, an attempt to de-escalate Red Sea/Suez disruption risks. Should this gain traction, the implied disinflationary impulse for global supply chains could soften the safe-haven bid in JPY, while leaving USD/JPY supported by relative yield dynamics. Separately, Canada's counter-tariff package of roughly $20 billion adds a layer of trade-policy uncertainty but is not a direct USD/JPY driver; the cross remains primarily a function of U.S. yields, BoJ policy optionality, and risk-on/off flows.
